# Prior-art boundary

Duty of Care does not claim to have invented media guidance on depicting
suicide, self-harm, or addiction, nor script review as a service.

- The World Health Organization publishes *Preventing suicide: a resource for
  filmmakers and others working on stage and screen* (2019):
  <https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/preventing-suicide-a-resource-for-filmmakers-and-others-working-on-stage-and-screen>
- Samaritans publishes drama guidance and runs a human Media Advisory service
  that reviews scripts on request:
  <https://www.samaritans.org/about-samaritans/media-guidelines/guidance-portrayals-suicide-and-self-harm-drama/>
- The National Action Alliance for Suicide Prevention, with SAMHSA and the
  Entertainment Industries Council, publishes the *National Recommendations for
  Depicting Suicide* (2019):
  <https://theactionalliance.org/resource/national-recommendations-depicting-suicide>
- The American Foundation for Suicide Prevention publishes Safe Storytelling
  guidance for creators.

Our documented search found published guidance documents and human advisory
services, but no software product that parses a screenplay, selects candidate
scenes deterministically, retrieves the applicable clause with its citation,
and leaves the decision with the writer. The incumbents are small teams with
finite capacity; this is a distribution gap, not a competitor. The product
routes writers toward those services where an expert view is warranted and
never presents itself as a substitute.

The contribution claimed is narrow: the no-citation-no-flag gate between a
deterministic trigger layer and a retrieval-grounded explanation, a hard output
filter that rejects any alternative more method-specific than the writer's own
text, and a product surface with no score and no blocking path. This is a
documented public gap, not proof that no private workflow exists.
