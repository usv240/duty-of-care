# Evidence base

Fourteen peer-reviewed records behind the guidance this tool cites, verified against Europe PMC on 2026-09-08 (Europe PMC REST records (title, authors, journal, year, DOI, PMID) and the published abstracts). Findings are paraphrased from the published abstracts with the numbers the abstracts report. The records live in a second Google Agent Search data store (`duty-of-care-evidence`) and are retrieved beside every note by trigger class; the site shows them at `/evidence` and serves them from `GET /v1/evidence`.

Nothing here is a claim that any single scene causes or prevents harm, that following guidance makes a script safe, or that this evidence base is complete. The studies that disagree are included on purpose and mapped to the same trigger classes as the studies they contest.

## Mechanisms

### Role of media reports in completed and prevented suicide: Werther v. Papageno effects

Niederkrotenthaler T, Voracek M, Herberth A, Till B, Strauss M, Etzersdorfer E, Eisenwort B, Sonneck G. *British Journal of Psychiatry*, 2010. doi:[10.1192/bjp.bp.109.074633](https://doi.org/10.1192/bjp.bp.109.074633) · PMID [20807970](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20807970/) · observational

**Finding.** Analysing Austrian print coverage against national suicide rates, reports of suicide, especially of celebrities, with repetitive coverage and detail, were associated with increases in suicides; coverage of people coping with adversity in a suicidal crisis was associated with decreases, which the authors named the Papageno effect.

**For a writer.** The same mechanism that makes method detail and repetition risky makes a depicted way through a crisis protective. A scene that shows coping is not a concession; it is the evidence-backed choice.

Trigger classes: method_specificity, framing_as_solution, absence_of_help_seeking, romanticisation, repetition, signposting_absence

## Fiction and film

### Systematic review and meta-analyses of suicidal outcomes following fictional portrayals of suicide and suicide attempt in entertainment media

Niederkrotenthaler T, Kirchner S, Till B, Sinyor M, Tran US, Pirkis J, Spittal MJ. *EClinicalMedicine*, 2021. doi:[10.1016/j.eclinm.2021.100922](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eclinm.2021.100922) · PMID [34308310](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34308310/) · meta-analysis

**Finding.** Pooling studies of fictional portrayals, depictions of suicide death were associated with an 18% increase in suicides (RR 1.18, 95% CI 1.09 to 1.27); portrayals of suicide attempts showed an increase that did not reach significance (RR 1.33, 95% CI 0.84 to 2.09).

**For a writer.** Fiction is not exempt from the effects first measured in news. How the act is depicted matters as much on screen as in print.

Trigger classes: method_specificity, framing_as_solution, romanticisation, repetition

### Papageno Stories Predict Lower Suicide Rates: Analysis of American Feature Films, 1950-2002

Stack S, Bowman B, Sinyor M, Niederkrotenthaler T. *Crisis*, 2026. doi:[10.1027/0227-5910/a001012](https://doi.org/10.1027/0227-5910/a001012) · PMID [40557937](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40557937/) · observational

**Finding.** Across American feature films from 1950 to 2002, each additional exposure to a recovery-focused film narrative was associated with a lower national suicide rate (b = -0.059, SE 0.023, p = 0.015), in a model explaining 89% of the variance.

**For a writer.** Stories of people coming through a crisis are associated with fewer deaths at population scale. The responsible-depiction preset is what that looks like in a script.

Trigger classes: absence_of_help_seeking, framing_as_solution, signposting_absence

### Determining the effects of films with suicidal content: a laboratory experiment

Till B, Strauss M, Sonneck G, Niederkrotenthaler T. *British Journal of Psychiatry*, 2015. doi:[10.1192/bjp.bp.114.152827](https://doi.org/10.1192/bjp.bp.114.152827) · PMID [25906792](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25906792/) · experiment

**Finding.** In a randomised laboratory experiment, a film ending in suicide increased suicidality among higher-vulnerability viewers who identified with the protagonist, while a film in which the protagonist copes increased life satisfaction, particularly among viewers with higher suicidal tendencies.

**For a writer.** Identification is the hinge. The more a vulnerable viewer identifies with the character, the more the ending matters: an ending in coping helps exactly the people an ending in death harms.

Trigger classes: framing_as_solution, absence_of_help_seeking, romanticisation

### Suicide in films: the impact of suicide portrayals on nonsuicidal viewers' well-being and the effectiveness of censorship

Till B, Niederkrotenthaler T, Herberth A, Vitouch P, Sonneck G. *Suicide and Life-Threatening Behavior*, 2010. doi:[10.1521/suli.2010.40.4.319](https://doi.org/10.1521/suli.2010.40.4.319) · PMID [20822358](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20822358/) · experiment

**Finding.** Watching films with suicide portrayals worsened mood and increased depression scores in non-suicidal viewers while, paradoxically, raising self-esteem and lowering suicidality; cutting the suicide scene did not remove the effects.

**For a writer.** Effects are mixed and are not removed by simply cutting the act. Context, framing, and aftermath carry the weight, which is why the guidance is about how, not whether.

Trigger classes: method_specificity, repetition, romanticisation

### Emergency department visits for self-harm in adolescents after release of the Netflix series '13 Reasons Why'

Sinyor M, Mallia E, de Oliveira C, Schaffer A, Niederkrotenthaler T, Zaheer J, Kurdyak P. *Australian and New Zealand Journal of Psychiatry*, 2022. doi:[10.1177/00048674211065999](https://doi.org/10.1177/00048674211065999) · PMID [34963338](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34963338/) · observational

**Finding.** In Ontario, adolescent emergency department visits for self-harm exceeded the expected count by an estimated 75 visits (6.4%) in the three months after the series' release.

**For a writer.** Outcomes short of death moved too. Self-harm portrayal deserves the same care as suicide portrayal, which is why the guidance this tool cites treats both.

Trigger classes: repetition, method_specificity

## Contested findings

### Association Between the Release of Netflix's 13 Reasons Why and Suicide Rates in the United States: An Interrupted Time Series Analysis

Bridge JA, Greenhouse JB, Ruch D, Stevens J, Ackerman J, Sheftall AH, Horowitz LM, Kelleher KJ, Campo JV. *Journal of the American Academy of Child and Adolescent Psychiatry*, 2020. doi:[10.1016/j.jaac.2019.04.020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jaac.2019.04.020) · PMID [31042568](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31042568/) · observational

**Finding.** In an interrupted time-series analysis, the series' release was associated with a significant increase in monthly suicide rates among 10- to 17-year-olds (IRR 1.29, 95% CI 1.09 to 1.53), a finding restricted to boys.

**For a writer.** The strongest published signal that a single fictional series can move population rates. It is also the study most argued over; see the reanalysis below.

Trigger classes: framing_as_solution, method_specificity, romanticisation

### Association of Increased Youth Suicides in the United States With the Release of 13 Reasons Why

Niederkrotenthaler T, Stack S, Till B, Sinyor M, Pirkis J, Garcia D, Rockett IRH, Tran US. *JAMA Psychiatry*, 2019. doi:[10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2019.0922](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2019.0922) · PMID [31141094](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31141094/) · observational

**Finding.** Using a different method over the same period, the authors estimated 66 excess suicides among 10- to 19-year-old males (a 12.4% increase) and 37 among females (a 21.7% increase) in the three months after release.

**For a writer.** Two independent teams, two methods, the same direction. The disagreement that follows is about attribution, not about whether the numbers moved.

Trigger classes: framing_as_solution, method_specificity, romanticisation

### Reanalysis of the Bridge et al. study of suicide following release of 13 Reasons Why

Romer D. *PLOS ONE*, 2020. doi:[10.1371/journal.pone.0227545](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0227545) · PMID [31945088](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31945088/) · reanalysis

**Finding.** Reanalysing the Bridge et al. data, Romer found the April increase in boys' suicides was no greater than the increase already observed in the month before the series was released, and concluded that harmful effects are difficult to attribute from aggregate rates.

**For a writer.** A tool about honesty has to carry the study that disagrees with its premise. The guidance still stands on the wider evidence; this is why the landing page says the evidence is contested.

Trigger classes: framing_as_solution, method_specificity, romanticisation

### Suicides in Young People in Ontario Following the Release of "13 Reasons Why"

Sinyor M, Williams M, Tran US, Schaffer A, Kurdyak P, Pirkis J, Niederkrotenthaler T. *Canadian Journal of Psychiatry*, 2019. doi:[10.1177/0706743719870507](https://doi.org/10.1177/0706743719870507) · PMID [31434503](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31434503/) · observational

**Finding.** In Ontario, 264 suicides were observed against 224 predicted among young people after release, about 40 additional deaths or an 18% increase.

**For a writer.** A Canadian signal in the same direction as the US studies, which is one reason the Canadian region in this tool carries its own guidance and helpline.

Trigger classes: framing_as_solution, romanticisation

## Do guidelines work

### Media roles in suicide prevention: a systematic review

Sisask M, Värnik A. *International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health*, 2012. doi:[10.3390/ijerph9010123](https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph9010123) · PMID [22470283](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22470283/) · systematic review

**Finding.** Reviewing 56 articles, the authors found stronger evidence for harmful imitation effects than for protective effects, and recommended that media guidelines be implemented and their effects evaluated.

**For a writer.** The protective side of the ledger is real but thinner. That asymmetry is why guidance leads with what to avoid, and why this tool never says a scene is safe.

Trigger classes: signposting_absence, absence_of_help_seeking, method_specificity

## Reviews and meta-analyses

### Association between suicide reporting in the media and suicide: systematic review and meta-analysis

Niederkrotenthaler T, Braun M, Pirkis J, Till B, Stack S, Sinyor M, Tran US, Voracek M, Cheng Q, Arendt F, Scherr S, Yip PSF, Spittal MJ. *BMJ*, 2020. doi:[10.1136/bmj.m575](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.m575) · PMID [32188637](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32188637/) · meta-analysis

**Finding.** Across studies of news reporting, reports of a celebrity suicide were associated with a 13% increase in suicides in the following one to two months (RR 1.13, 95% CI 1.08 to 1.18); reporting the method used was associated with a 30% increase in deaths by the same method (RR 1.30, 95% CI 1.18 to 1.44).

**For a writer.** Method detail is the best-evidenced risk factor in the literature. It is why every guidance document leads with it, and why this tool's filter refuses to add any.

Trigger classes: method_specificity, repetition, romanticisation

### Suicide in the media: a quantitative review of studies based on non-fictional stories

Stack S. *Suicide and Life-Threatening Behavior*, 2005. doi:[10.1521/suli.35.2.121.62877](https://doi.org/10.1521/suli.35.2.121.62877) · PMID [15843330](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15843330/) · quantitative review

**Finding.** Across 419 findings from 55 studies, studies of celebrity suicides were 5.27 times more likely to report a copycat effect, and studies of real rather than fictional suicides were 4.03 times more likely to find one.

**For a writer.** Real, prominent, repeated stories carry the most risk; fiction carries less on average, but not none. It is why repetition is a trigger class here and why the tool compares, rather than equates, fiction with news.

Trigger classes: repetition, romanticisation

### Suicide and the media. Part II: Portrayal in fictional media

Pirkis J, Blood RW. *Crisis*, 2001. doi:[10.1027//0227-5910.22.4.155](https://doi.org/10.1027//0227-5910.22.4.155) · PMID [11848659](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11848659/) · review

**Finding.** Reviewing studies of fictional portrayals in film, television, and books, the authors found the evidence for imitation effects less consistent than for news, with methodological limits, but sufficient to warrant caution about how suicide is portrayed in fiction.

**For a writer.** Twenty-five years of caution about fiction, before the streaming-era studies. The guidance this tool cites grew out of exactly this literature.

Trigger classes: method_specificity, framing_as_solution, romanticisation, repetition
